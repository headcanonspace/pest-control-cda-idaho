Signs and Identification

How to Tell If You Have a Spider Problem

Seeing an occasional spider does not always mean you have a serious infestation. Repeated sightings, multiple webs, egg sacs, or spiders appearing in the same areas can indicate that your property is providing shelter or access to insects they feed on.

Common signs of spider activity include:

  • Spiders appearing repeatedly inside the home
  • Webs around windows, doors, corners, and ceilings
  • Webs around eaves, porches, and exterior walls
  • Spiders in garages, sheds, crawl spaces, or storage areas
  • Egg sacs attached to webs or sheltered surfaces
  • Increased spider activity around exterior lights
  • Spiders appearing near landscaping, woodpiles, or other sheltered areas
  • Multiple webs returning after they have been removed

Spider identification can also matter because different species can prefer different locations and conditions. If you are unsure what type of spider you are seeing, identifying the activity helps determine where to inspect and what treatment approach may be appropriate.

Local Activity Factors

Why Spiders Show Up in Coeur d'Alene

Spiders generally look for food, shelter, and protected places to build webs or remain undisturbed. Properties that have plenty of insects, vegetation, outdoor lighting, cracks, gaps, or sheltered areas may experience more noticeable spider activity.

Here is what that can mean locally:

Homes Near Lake Coeur d'Alene

Homes and properties around the lake may have decks, landscaping, outdoor structures, vegetation, and other sheltered areas that can support insect and spider activity.

Garages, Sheds & Storage Areas

Garages, sheds, crawl spaces, and storage areas provide dark and relatively undisturbed locations where spiders can build webs and remain sheltered.

Exterior Lights

Outdoor lights can attract flying insects. When insects gather around doors, windows, porches, and exterior lights, spiders may also become more noticeable in those areas.

Landscaping & Vegetation

Shrubs, branches, ground cover, and vegetation close to a structure can provide shelter and may give insects and spiders easier access to the building.

Cracks, Gaps & Entry Points

Spiders may enter through gaps around doors, windows, utility openings, foundations, and other accessible areas. Finding these areas can be an important part of understanding recurring indoor activity.

Proactive Protection

Spider Prevention Tips & Seasonal Calendar

Simple habits around your property can help reduce the conditions that attract spiders and the insects they feed on.

Six Tips You Can Do Yourself

1. Remove Webs

Remove visible webs from accessible corners, windows, eaves, garages, sheds, and other areas where they regularly appear.

2. Seal Entry Points

Check gaps around doors, windows, pipes, utility openings, and foundation areas. Seal accessible openings where appropriate.

3. Manage Outdoor Lighting

Where practical, reduce unnecessary exterior lighting near doors and windows because lights can attract insects that spiders feed on.

4. Trim Vegetation

Keep branches, shrubs, and other vegetation from directly touching the structure where practical.

5. Reduce Outdoor Clutter

Move unnecessary boxes, stored materials, wood, and debris away from the structure when possible. Reducing undisturbed areas can make spider activity easier to identify.

6. Reduce Insect Activity

Spiders feed on insects, so reducing the insects around your property can also reduce some of the food sources that support spider activity.

When Spider Activity Is Highest

Spiders can appear throughout the year. What changes is their activity, available food, shelter, and movement around buildings.

Spring

Increasing Outdoor Activity

As temperatures rise, insects and other outdoor activity increase. Spiders may become more noticeable around landscaping, windows, garages, and exterior structures.

Summer

More Visible Web & Outdoor Activity

Warm conditions can lead to increased spider and insect activity around porches, eaves, windows, gardens, garages, and other outdoor areas.

Fall

Searching for Shelter

As outdoor temperatures become cooler, spiders may become more noticeable around buildings and protected areas as they seek shelter.

Winter

Protected Indoor Areas

Spider activity may still be noticed indoors in garages, basements, crawl spaces, storage areas, and other sheltered locations.

Start by identifying where spiders and webs are appearing and look for conditions that may be supporting them. Sealing accessible entry points, managing vegetation, reducing insect activity, and addressing recurring areas can help. Professional treatment may be appropriate when activity continues.
