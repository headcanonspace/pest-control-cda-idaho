Signs & Identification

How to Tell If You Have a Flea Problem

Flea infestations are not always easy to see. Fleas are small, move quickly, and can remain in carpets, pet bedding, furniture, cracks, and other protected areas.

Common signs include:

  • Small red or itchy bites on people
  • Pets scratching, licking, or biting their skin frequently
  • Tiny jumping insects around floors or furniture
  • Flea dirt that looks like small dark specks
  • Fleas around pet bedding and resting areas
  • Increased flea activity after pets spend time outdoors
  • Fleas in carpets, rugs, upholstery, and floor cracks
  • Flea activity in areas where pets regularly sleep or rest

Finding fleas on a pet does not necessarily mean the problem is limited to the pet. Flea eggs and immature stages can remain in the surrounding environment, which is why treating the affected areas is an important part of flea control.

Proactive Protection

Flea Prevention Tips

Professional treatment can help address an existing infestation, while ongoing prevention can help reduce the chance of flea activity returning.

Treat Pets Through Your Veterinarian

If your pet has fleas, discuss appropriate flea-control options with your veterinarian. Pets can be an important part of managing a flea problem.

Wash Pet Bedding

Regularly washing pet bedding can help remove fleas and flea-related debris from frequently used areas.

Vacuum Regularly

Vacuum carpets, rugs, furniture, cracks, and areas where pets spend time. Pay particular attention to pet resting areas.

Clean Pet Resting Areas

Keep areas where dogs and cats sleep or rest clean and regularly maintained.

Reduce Outdoor Exposure

Be aware of areas where pets may encounter fleas outdoors and monitor pets after outdoor activity.

Keep Floors & Carpets Maintained

Regular cleaning can help reduce debris and provide better visibility of potential flea activity.

Help & Answers

Flea Control FAQs

Direct, practical answers about residential flea control in Coeur d'Alene.

Ask a Specialist: (208) 248-2701
How do I know if my house has fleas?

Common signs include itchy bites, pets scratching frequently, flea dirt, and small jumping insects around carpets, furniture, pet bedding, and other resting areas.

Can fleas live in carpet?

Yes. Carpets and rugs can provide protected areas where flea eggs and immature stages may remain.

Can fleas come from my pets?

Pets can carry fleas indoors after outdoor exposure or contact with infested animals or environments. However, flea activity can also continue in the surrounding environment.

How much does flea control cost in Coeur d'Alene?

The cost depends on factors such as property size, infestation level, affected rooms, flooring, pet areas, and the treatment recommended after inspection.

Why do fleas keep coming back?

Recurring flea activity can be associated with continued exposure, untreated areas, pets, or flea life stages remaining in the environment.

Do I need to treat my pet too?

If your pet has fleas, speak with your veterinarian about appropriate flea-control options. Managing the pet and the surrounding environment may both be important.

How can I prevent fleas?

Regular vacuuming, washing pet bedding, maintaining pet resting areas, monitoring pets after outdoor activity, and addressing flea problems early can help reduce the risk of continued activity.

Can fleas affect people?

Fleas can bite people, often causing small itchy spots. If you are experiencing persistent or severe skin symptoms, consult a healthcare professional.
