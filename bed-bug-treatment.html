Signs and Identification

How to Tell If You Have Bed Bugs

Adult bed bugs are flat, reddish brown, and about the size of an apple seed. They turn purplish red after feeding. They are not a sign of poor hygiene, and they can affect anyone.

  • Live bugs in mattress seams, box springs, and furniture joints
  • Rusty or dark red spots on sheets and mattresses
  • Dark dots that look like pen marks, which are droppings
  • Shed skins left after molting
  • Pale, tiny young bugs, as small as a pinhead
  • Bites in a line or cluster on exposed skin, such as the face, neck, arms, and hands

Bites alone are not proof. The CDC says reactions vary, and some people show none.

Many things get mistaken for bed bugs. WSU Extension says similar bugs infest bats, swallows, and poultry. Bites from mosquitoes and other insects look alike. University researchers also found that most samples sent in for identification were not bed bugs. Correct identification comes first.

Local Activity Factors

Why Bed Bugs Show Up in Coeur d'Alene

Bed bugs do not follow the weather or the forest, as ants and mice do. They follow people. WSU Extension says they ride in luggage, clothing, bedding, and upholstered furniture. A publication from Oregon State, Washington State, and University of Idaho Extension says they hitchhike on secondhand furniture and clothing, and can sit unnoticed for months before you find them.

Here is what that means locally:

Downtown & Lakefront Lodging

Lodging and short term rentals downtown and by the lake change guests often. They fill up for the Fourth of July Festival, Ironman weekend, and the Holiday Light Show.

Seasonal Cabins Around Hayden Lake

Seasonal cabins around Hayden Lake sit empty, then fill with travelers' bags as families arrive for vacations.

Apartments & Townhomes

Apartments and townhomes in Coeur d'Alene and Post Falls share walls, and bed bugs can travel between rooms and floors through electrical conduits and wall gaps.

Frequent Travelers

Frequent travelers may bring bugs home from hotels. WSU reports that bed bug cases on its campus came mostly from student apartments, because of the high rate of travel.

Proactive Protection

Prevention Tips & Seasonal Calendar

Simple habits you can practice at home and how bed bug entry patterns shift throughout the year.

Six Tips You Can Do Yourself

1. Travel Inspection

When you travel, check headboards, mattress seams, baseboards, and picture frames before you bring bags in. Keep luggage off the bed and the floor.

2. Post-Trip Laundry

When you get home, wash travel clothes right away on high heat and store luggage in sealed plastic bags, away from the bedroom.

3. Secondhand Checks

Check used furniture thoroughly before bringing it in. Never bring in a discarded roadside mattress or upholstered couch.

4. Zippered Encasements

Use zippered encasements on your mattress and box spring. The EPA recommends leaving them sealed for at least a full year.

5. Frequent Vacuuming

Vacuum mattress seams, headboards, and floor cracks regularly. Always empty or seal the vacuum bag in outdoor trash immediately.

6. Cut Bedroom Clutter

Remove boxes, clothing piles, and items stored under beds so insects have fewer harborage zones and inspections remain easy.

When the Risk Is Highest

Bed bugs live indoors, so they can show up any month. What changes is how they arrive.

Spring

Moving Season

Increased tenant turnover in rentals, college dorm move-outs, and garage sales with secondhand furniture.

Summer

Peak Travel & Vacation Rentals

WSU says warmer weather brings more travel and a higher chance of picking up bed bugs from fully booked lake rentals and hotels.

Fall

Closing Up Seasonal Cabins

Time to winterize lakefront properties. Inspect mattresses and store linens in sealed plastic tubs before locking up for the season.

Winter

Holiday Travel & Light Show Guests

Out-of-town holiday guests, family travel, and visitors in Coeur d'Alene for the Holiday Light Show increase hitchhiking opportunities.

Help & Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Direct, practical answers from our licensed pest control professionals.

Ask a Specialist: (208) 248-2701
How much does bed bug treatment cost in Coeur d'Alene?

We provide a free inspection and clear written estimate before any work starts. Price depends on the size of the property, how bad the problem is, the method, and the number of visits, so there are no surprises.

How long does it take to get rid of bed bugs?

Most jobs need more than one visit over several weeks. Eggs and hidden bugs can outlast one treatment, and WSU says adult bed bugs can live up to six months without feeding. That is why we recheck and treat again if we see activity.

Is bed bug treatment safe for pets and kids?

We follow the product label strictly, which sets where a product goes and how long to stay out. Tell us about pets, kids, birds, and fish tanks before we start. We tell you exactly when it is safe to return to treated rooms.

Can I do it myself, or do I need a professional?

Early cases may improve with hot laundry, encasements, interceptors, and vacuuming. But the NPIC says foggers only kill bugs out in the open, and WSU Extension says bed bugs are difficult to eliminate. If you cannot find every hiding spot, call a licensed professional.

How do I keep bed bugs from coming back?

Check hotel beds when you travel. Wash travel clothes right away and store luggage in sealed bags. Inspect used furniture, keep encasements on for a year, vacuum seams, and cut clutter. Tell your landlord or manager at once if you share walls.

What guarantee do you offer?

Because bed bug control requires thoroughness and cooperation, we clearly put re-treatment warranty terms in writing before we start, including what it covers, how long it lasts, and scheduled follow-ups.

Do I need to throw away my mattress or furniture?

Usually not. The EPA advises against throwing out a mattress and recommends zippered encasements instead. If you must discard furniture, wrap it in plastic first so bugs do not spread through the house.

Are bed bugs dangerous?

The CDC says bed bugs are not known to spread disease. Bites can itch, cause sleeplessness, and allergic reactions are rare. See a doctor if you have a severe reaction or an infected bite.
