Common Pest Problems Here

Sagle sits about five miles south of Sandpoint, across the Long Bridge, with Lake Pend Oreille on one side and forest on the other. Snow, damp sills, and shaded lots shape the pests here.

Carpenter ants in damp sills and subfloors

University of Idaho Extension calls carpenter ants serious building pests in northern Idaho, where moisture is plentiful. The National Pesticide Information Center says they prefer damp wood and that finding the nest is the key to control. Snow stacked against a foundation can keep wood wet into spring, so we check sills, crawl space framing, and decks first.

Packrats and mice in crawl spaces and cabins

Idaho Fish and Game lists the bushy tailed woodrat, or packrat, as present and native in Idaho, and woodrats may move into the attics and walls of houses near their habitat and damage wiring. Idaho's Central District Health says the main hantavirus concern in Idaho is Sin Nombre virus, carried by deer mice. We seal vents and gaps with heavy gauge hardware cloth, then trap.

Spiders in damp basements and crawl vents

Most are harmless. Washington State University warns that spraying alone is a short fix unless you stop spiders from getting in. We remove webs and egg sacs, seal gaps, and treat the spots they use.

Wasps on sunny siding and eaves

Check eaves, decks, and cedar or log walls in spring for small nests, and call us for any nest near a door.

Cabins that sit empty

Seal gaps in fall and check for nests and droppings in spring. When you reopen, air the cabin out first, and do not sweep or vacuum droppings, as University of Arizona Extension advises. We offer comprehensive close up checks in fall and open up checks in spring.

Ticks

Panhandle Health District says North Idaho has several tick types that can spread disease, and ticks live in tall grass, brush, and wooded areas. Keep grass short near the house and check pets and kids after time outdoors.

Common Questions

Have questions about pest treatments, safety, or our warranty? Here are answers from our licensed Idaho technicians.

How do you stop packrats and damp subfloor pests in lake homes and cabins?

We find how they get in and close it with heavy gauge hardware cloth over crawl vents and gaps. We set snap traps along their travel routes, remove nesting debris, and look for the moisture that draws carpenter ants and other pests. Packrats can chew wiring, so we check for damage and tell you if an electrician should look.

We follow the product label, which sets where a product goes, how long to stay out, and limits near water. Tell us about kids, pets, docks, wells, and the shoreline before we start. We explain every product we use.
